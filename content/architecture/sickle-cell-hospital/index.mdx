The Sickle Cell Specialist Hospital is a conceptual architectural proposal for a specialist healthcare facility in Ile-Ife, Osun State, Nigeria. It is presented as a design proposal, not a constructed building.

## Concept

The form of the building is inspired by the distinctive shape of a sickled red blood cell. That biological form informs the building’s geometry and the arrangement of its curved wings: two crescent-shaped wings, joined at the centre by a circular core.

The result is a building whose shape carries the identity of the condition it is designed to serve.

## Design philosophy

The design sets out to create a healing environment that considers both the physical and the psychosocial needs of people living with sickle cell disease.

Key considerations:

- **Natural lighting.**
- **Landscaped surroundings**, with planting and trees around the building.
- **Patient-centred planning**, organising spaces around the people who use them.
- **Functional healthcare spaces.**

## Spatial planning

The building is planned over a ground floor and three upper floors. On every level the two curved wings meet at the central circular core. The plans below show how each level is arranged, followed by the roof plan, section and elevation.
